Aeternal

A turn-based deck-builder roguelike set in a world of Greek gods. A mortal ruler mocks Death, and pays for it.

How combat works

Combat is positional. The enemy stands in the center of a 3×3 grid, and you stand on one of the four squares around it. Each round the enemy marks the square(s) it will attack, so you have to move out of the way with movement cards and then strike back.

Systems

Telegraphed intents

Enemies announce their next attack direction and damage before it happens.

Action Points and Divine Focus

AP is spent to play cards each round. Divine Focus is spent to shift to another deck with its own effects.

Status effects

Bleed deals damage at the end of a round and stacks. Stun and Blackout skip an action. Dodge cancels the next attack.

Run structure

Each fight rewards a new card. Health carries over between fights, and losing ends the run.

Level 1: The Fall

The prologue of the game, set during the empire's tenth-year feast.

  1. Opening narrative: the birth of Time and Death, and the mortal Chronus blessed.
  2. Goblin: a feast-day challenger.
  3. Bandit Champion (elite): tougher, and attacks three squares at once.
  4. Thanatos: a scripted boss. He cannot be brought below 1 HP, and enrages on round 3 or at half health.

The level is still in progress. The epilogue and later regions are not implemented yet.

Under the hood

Data-driven encounters

Each fight is defined in a ScriptableObject (EncounterData), so one scene can host many enemies.

Event-driven combat

Combat, UI and status updates communicate through an event bus instead of direct references.

Scale

About 9,700 lines of C# in the game scripts, plus editor setup tools for building the scenes.
